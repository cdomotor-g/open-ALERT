open-ALERT Mallow trial

Does a Toradex Verdin AM62 on a Mallow carrier — industrial parts, no Raspberry Pi — do the job of the Pi 4 you already have? Run the two side by side on the same antenna for two weeks and find out. What to buy, what to do, what to compare, and how it passes.

The set-up

One antenna, split two ways, so both computers hear exactly the same bursts. Each has its own stick of the same model, its own Flood-Net ingest token and its own receivers in Flood-Net. Flood-Net keeps one copy of each reading — whichever arrives first — but records every copy on the Reception Map, so the two can be compared burst for burst. Halfway through, swap the sticks: no two sticks are equally good, and swapping cancels that out.

Passes if

  • it decodes at least 98% of the bursts the Pi does, over both weeks
  • no channel ever drops samples
  • Decoder busy stays under 70% in the worst hour
  • the processor stays under 70 °C
  • no restarts or check-in gaps of its own

Takes

  • a couple of hours on the bench to bring it up
  • an hour on site to fit the splitter
  • two weeks running — ideally with some rain in them
  • an hour to read off and compare the numbers

Which module

Toradex makes six Verdin AM62s. They all use the same Cortex-A53 cores — the very cores of a Raspberry Pi 3B+ — so what differs is how many and how fast. Each is rated below against what RPi ALERT 0.17's lean decoder asks of it, and what it does with 0.18 filtering only around bursts (below). Part numbers are Toradex's current ones. Prices are element14 Australia's, including GST, with Toradex's own direct price (to businesses only) under each — element14 charges two to three times as much for the modules.

Will it keep up?

The AM62's four Cortex-A53 cores are about half a Pi 4 for decoding. RPi ALERT 0.17's lean decoder takes a third of the time the old one did over a burst. Put in the channels your Pi listens on to see what the builder's model expects — then let the trial say what really happens.

A lighter decoder

RPi ALERT's decoder is Flood-Net's own, so the box decodes exactly as the website does. Two changes make it lighter, both measured against the decoder RPi ALERT ran until 0.16:

Filter once per guess — done, 0.17's default

A burst's decode tries 45 carrier and timing guesses and used to re-filter the whole window for each. Now it filters once for each of the 9 carrier guesses and makes the ±75 and ±150 Hz nudges afterwards, at 12 kHz. At the Brisbane base, run both ways overnight: 14 of 11,628 live bursts read differently (0.12%), in a third of the time. 0.17 also fills its buffers a block at a time and works out a stick's spectrum on one channel only: a quarter to two fifths less between bursts.

Filter only around bursts — built, 0.18, being checked

Between bursts, every channel used to filter the stick's whole window all the time. Now each can keep the last 1.6 s as bytes, look at its channel 25 times a second, and filter only from a second before something shows, so the squelch and the decoder see the samples they always did. In Flood-Net's tests, the same readings and the same bursts every time. Asleep, a channel costs a quarter of filtering always at 240 kHz and a fifteenth at 2.4 MHz. It's Settings → RTL-SDR → Between bursts, off by default until its Check has run at the Brisbane base.

What it means

On 0.17, Flood-Net's four channels on a busy day take about a third of the Quad and two thirds of the cheaper Dual. The Quad keeps the headroom for a site that hears a whole region, which is why it's the one to trial. Filtering only around bursts brings those to about a fifth and two fifths. On a heavy day bursts keep it awake most of the time, so it saves little there.

What to do

  1. Before anything arrives: write down the Pi's week

    The splitter will cost the Pi about 3.5 dB. So for two or three days before you fit it, note what the Pi does on its own — that's your baseline:

    • On the Pi's Overview (http://<the Pi>/): bursts decoded per channel, Decoder busy, any dropped samples, the processor temperature.
    • On its Receivers page: the SNR of two or three stations it hears well.
    • In Flood-Net: its receivers on the Reception Map, and its row on the Base Stations tab.
    • On the Pi, over SSH: rpi-alert sdr 1 lists its channels, gain and ppm — you'll give the Mallow the same.

  2. On the bench: put the module on the carrier

    1. With the power off, slide the Verdin module into Mallow's edge connector at an angle, press it down flat and fit its screw. Fit the heatsink over it with its thermal pad.
    2. Connect Ethernet to a network with internet, then 12 V to the power header (mind the polarity marked on the board).
    3. Power on. A new module comes with Toradex Easy Installer on it: find its IP address (your router's list of devices, or a monitor on the HDMI port) and open it in a browser or a VNC viewer.
    4. Install Torizon OS (the plain one, without the evaluation containers). Power off when it says Image installed, then on again.
    5. Log in over SSH: ssh torizon@<its IP> — the first password is torizon, and it makes you choose a new one. Give the box a name you'll recognise in Flood-Net: sudo hostnamectl set-hostname am62-trial, then sudo reboot.

    No Easy Installer? Load it again in recovery mode from a computer over USB-C — Toradex: loading Toradex Easy Installer — and see If it won't below.

  3. On the bench: run RPi ALERT in a container

    Torizon runs software in Docker containers, and RPi ALERT's installer is made for Raspberry Pi OS — so the agent runs in a Debian container instead. This has not been done before: it is the first time RPi ALERT runs off a Raspberry Pi, and part of what the trial finds out. Log in and make the image:

    mkdir -p ~/rpi-alert && cd ~/rpi-alert
cat > Dockerfile <<'EOF'
FROM debian:trixie-slim
RUN apt-get update \
 && apt-get install -y --no-install-recommends nodejs rtl-sdr git ca-certificates procps \
 && rm -rf /var/lib/apt/lists/*
RUN git clone --depth 1 https://github.com/cdomotor-g/RPi_ALERT /opt/rpi-alert
CMD ["node", "/opt/rpi-alert/agent/bin/rpi-alert", "daemon"]
EOF
docker build -t rpi-alert .

    Plug the new stick into a USB port, then start it — it restarts itself after a power cut, and keeps its settings and queue across updates:

    docker run -d --name rpi-alert --restart unless-stopped \
  --privileged --network host \
  -v /dev/bus/usb:/dev/bus/usb \
  -v rpi-alert-etc:/etc/rpi-alert -v rpi-alert-var:/var/lib/rpi-alert \
  rpi-alert
docker logs -f rpi-alert
    1. The log should show the stick found and rtl_sdr started. Then open http://<its IP>/ — the same dashboard as the Pi's.
    2. Decoder (Settings → RTL-SDR): leave both on Lean, RPi ALERT 0.17's default, so the two computers decode alike. A Pi on 0.15 or older gets it from Settings → System → Check for updates (or sudo rpi-alert-update over SSH).
    3. Give it the Pi's channels, gain and ppm (Settings → RTL-SDR), or: docker exec rpi-alert node /opt/rpi-alert/agent/bin/rpi-alert sdr 1 freq 151.5, 151.525, 151.95, 152.4 — with the Pi's own list. Leave the screen dashboard off: the Pi's comparison should be like for like, so turn the Pi's off too if it has one.
    4. Press Request a token, check the code, and approve it on Flood-Net's Admin tab. Its receivers appear as rpi-am62-trial-sdr1… beside the Pi's.
    5. Leave it on the bench a day with an antenna by a window. If Decoder busy is already near 100% here, it won't cope on site — see Will it keep up? and take some channels off before going on.

    Things that need the Pi's own system — the Wi-Fi hotspot, updates from Flood-Net, the screen — won't work in the container. That's expected; the trial is about receiving and decoding.

  4. On site: share the antenna

    1. Undo the coax at the Pi's stick. Between the arrestor and the sticks, fit the splitter: the antenna lead to its input (with the N–N jumper if needed), a pigtail from each output to each stick.
    2. Both boxes on the same switch or router, by Ethernet. Each on its power meter.
    3. Check both are hearing: each Overview counts bursts within minutes, and both sets of receivers turn up on Flood-Net's Reception Map.
    4. Write down the date and time — week 1 starts now. Stick A is on the Pi, stick B on the Mallow.

  5. After a week: swap the sticks

    Move stick A to the Mallow and stick B to the Pi (each keeps its own pigtail). Give each stick its own ppm on its new box — the tuning help on each stick's card suggests it within an hour. Write down the time: week 2 starts.

  6. After two weeks: read off the numbers

    Fill in the results sheet below from each box and from Flood-Net. The verdicts work themselves out. Then decide: keep the Mallow on site beside the Pi, take it off and keep the Pi, or — if it passes — let the Mallow take over.

If it won't

No Easy Installer screen, or it won't load over USB
Others have had this with an AM62 on Mallow: use the newest Toradex Easy Installer and the recovery steps in Toradex's guide, and ask on the Toradex Community — Toradex staff answer there. The console cable on Mallow's console header shows what it's doing.
The stick isn't found in the container
On the box, lsusb should list a Realtek RTL2838 device. If dmesg shows a DVB driver taking it, unload it (sudo modprobe -r dvb_usb_rtl28xxu) — RPi ALERT blacklists it on a Pi. Check the container was started with -v /dev/bus/usb:/dev/bus/usb and --privileged.
Decoder busy near 100%, or channels dropping samples
It is doing more than the AM62 can with RPi ALERT's decoder as it is. Take channels off until it settles, write down how many it managed — that is a result in itself — and see the builder's custom path: the faster decoder is what would make up the difference.
Hot: the processor over 70 °C
Check the heatsink is seated with its pad, and that the box isn't in the sun. Industrial-rated parts run hot without failing, but a throttled processor decodes less.
The Pi got worse after the splitter
Expected, by about 3.5 dB — compare with the baseline you wrote down. If a weak station drops out altogether, note it; take the splitter out when the trial ends.

When the trial ends

  • Take the splitter out to give the Pi its full signal back — or keep both running.
  • If the Mallow comes off, revoke its ingest token on Flood-Net's Admin tab and remove its receivers.
  • Put the results in RPi ALERT's issues so the builder's CPU model can use real AM62 numbers.
