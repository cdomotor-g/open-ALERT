# R4 verification — Australian government IT security report, checked by a second reader

Checked on 2026-11-11 against `(working folder, not kept)/R4-au-gov-it-security.md`.

## Result in short

- **23 of the 25 facts stand as written.** I opened the source for each one myself. Two stand only in part (facts 13 and 25, below).
- **Nothing in the 25 facts is false.** Three need a small correction of date, source or wording (facts 1, 11, 12).
- **One conclusion the report builds on fact 22 does not hold.** The report says the IEC 62443-4-2 certified class is "at or below" a Raspberry Pi 4. That is true of the Moxa models it looked at, and false of the class: the ISASecure register lists Intel Core industrial computers running Ubuntu 22.04 LTS, certified at the same level.
- **The report missed six things that bear on the decision** (section 3).

## 3. What the report missed

**1. Certified industrial computers with real processing power exist, and one family runs Ubuntu LTS.** (fetched)
The report surveyed Moxa and concluded the certified class is under-powered. The ISASecure register of IEC 62443-4-2 certified components lists, for Eurotech: "Modular, AI-Ready, Edge Servers for Industrial Applications — ReliaCOR 40-13 & 54-13 family — ESF 7.4.x Ubuntu Linux 22.04.x — CSA 1.0.0 Level 2 — 7/30/2024". A German reseller (InoNet) describes the 40-13 as a "Compact embedded PC with 13th generation Intel® Core™ i CPU". The earlier ReliaCOR 44-11 certificate (EUTC-RCOR4411-CSA-E01, issued 16 February 2024) reads "Capability Security Level 2 requirements for host device", with "Ubuntu Linux 22.04.3 LTS" and "ESF 7.3.1". That puts the report's rank-1 operating system and its rank-2 certificate in one box, on a processor far above a Pi 4.
Caveats, all open:
- The certificate covers Eurotech's own build (Ubuntu plus its ESF framework). As with Moxa, adding Node.js and `rtl_sdr` is the buyer's change.
- The product certificate is valid only while Eurotech's development-process certificate is. That certificate (EUTC-SEPDP-SDLA-E01, fetched) says "valid until the expiration date of October 11, 2026" — a month ago. Eurotech received new product certificates in June and August 2026, so a renewal is likely, but I did not find it. Ask Eurotech.
- No Australian stockist or price found. Ubuntu 22.04 standard support ends in 2027 (memory, unchecked); the certificate is for 22.04, not 24.04 or 26.04.
- Advantech also announced (22 October 2026, fetched) a DEKRA IEC 62443-4-2 SL2 attestation for its AFE-E630, "Rockchip RK3568J", Debian-based — another certified Arm box, but again a modest processor.
URLs: https://isasecure.org/end-users/iec-62443-4-2-certified-components ; https://isasecure.org/hubfs/EUTC-RCOR4411-CSA-E01.pdf ; https://isasecure.org/hubfs/EUTC-SEPDP-SDLA-E01.pdf ; https://www.advantech.com/en/resources/news/afe-e630-iec62443-dekra

**2. Australia adopted IEC 62443 as an Australian Standard in July 2025.** (fetched)
Standards Australia, 10 July 2025: "Australia has officially adopted the AS IEC 62443 series as national standards", naming water among the services it protects. The report says of IEC 62443 "Nobody by law in Australia" — still true — but a utility can now cite an Australian Standard number in a tender, which raises the weight of a certificate with operational-technology teams. The risk-program rules also allow "an equivalent" framework.
URL: https://www.standards.org.au/news/standards-australia-adopts-worlds-foremost-standard-for-operational-technology

**3. Toradex does state end dates — and they are short.** (fetched)
The report says Toradex gives no support duration. That is true of torizon.io/security. Toradex's developer site has a lifecycle table: version 7 "Active", released October 2024, "Projected EOL 2028"; version 6 "Maintenance", EOL 2026; version 5 "EOL" 2024; version 8 "Coming Soon", November 2026, EOL 2030; "Extended EOL: Contact Toradex". The page adds that the table "does not define the lifecycle dates or support status of Torizon OS 8". So each major version gets about four years, against ISM-1501 ("Operating systems that are no longer supported by vendors are replaced") and Ubuntu's ten or more. This strengthens the report's case against Torizon OS and gives the owner a figure to quote.
URL: https://developer.toradex.com/software/toradex-embedded-software/toradex-embedded-linux-support-strategy/

**4. Canonical holds an IEC 62443-4-1 certificate for its development process.** (fetched)
18 September 2025: "Canonical is proud to announce it has achieved compliance with IEC 62443-4-1"; "The audit for the IEC 62443-4-1 certification was conducted by TÜV SÜD." The article does not list Ubuntu Server as a certified product. It still answers the ISM's "demonstrated a commitment to the security of their products" (ISM-1568) for the operating-system supplier, and supports the report's Ubuntu-first advice.
URL: https://ubuntu.com/blog/canonical-iec-62443-4-1

**5. Raspberry Pi has signed a statement of compliance under the UK law the Australian smart-device rules copy.** (fetched)
Dated 13 June 2024, signed by its Chief Technology Officer – Software: "all variants of the following products are in compliance with the Act and Regulation": Raspberry Pi 4 Model B, 400, Compute Module 4, 5. "Our products meet the baseline requirements specified by the Standard" (ETSI EN 303 645). It is a self-declaration, not a third-party certificate, and it gives no end date for updates. Its companion paper says: "As a manufacturer of general-purpose computing products, Raspberry Pi is not directly responsible for ensuring that products meet the requirements of the PSTI… Designers who integrate a Raspberry Pi product into their equipment are likely to have to meet the requirements". So whoever sells a finished base station owns the support-period promise, on any board.
URLs: https://pip-assets.raspberrypi.com/categories/1004-cyber-security/documents/RP-006172-CF-1-rpi-cysec%20Cyber%20Security%20Declaration.pdf ; https://pip-assets.raspberrypi.com/categories/1260-security/documents/RP-007501-WP-1-rpi-compliance%20UK%20Cybersecurity%20for%20consumer%20Internet%20of%20Things%20(IoT).pdf

**6. Two state-level facts that change who is bound.** (fetched)
- Queensland has its own banned-products list: "Restricted applications, products and web services policy", v1.0.1, effective April 2025. It binds departments and statutory bodies; "Other Queensland public sector entities including councils and government-owned corporations are strongly encouraged to follow this guidance voluntarily." The list has three entries: TikTok, DeepSeek, Kaspersky Lab. No hardware.
- The NSW policy's own glossary lists Sydney Water and Hunter Water as state-owned corporations, so the NSW Cyber Security Policy is not mandatory for the two largest NSW water utilities. What binds them is the Commonwealth critical-infrastructure rules (facts 15–16).
URLs: the Queensland policy page above; https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-06/nsw-cyber-security-policy-2026-2027.pdf

**Smaller points.**
- Ubuntu Pro now offers 15 years with a "Legacy add-on" (fetched, ubuntu.com/pro). The report says 10.
- A search summary mentioned a 2026 water-sector submission about utilities with 100,000–200,000 connections, which suggests the threshold is being discussed (snippet only; not followed up).
- The report's ranking leans on Microsoft Defender and Tenable. It says so, and says to ask the customer which tools they run. That question should come before any hardware purchase aimed at a particular customer.
